Appearance

Anoures is a slim, athletic Skyborne in his mid twenties, built more for climbing and running than for brawling. His olive skin has the faint, wind-burnt look of someone raised up high, and his dark hair is kept neat but forgettable, the sort of cut nobody remembers an hour later. He moves lightly and easily, always balanced, as if he could be somewhere else in a heartbeat. Often he is.

His clothes are clean, well fitted and deliberately unremarkable: muted colours, soft boots, nothing that catches the light or the eye. People tend to describe him afterwards as "pleasant" and then struggle to say anything more. That is exactly the point. What they never notice are the blades, the vials and the folded papers tucked into places a tailor would never think to put a pocket.

Biography

The highlands. Anoures was the only child of a quiet couple in the Skyborne highlands, where the wind never stops and everyone knows everyone. He loved his mother and father, and he still does, but the place was terribly small for a boy who was born listening. He learned early that he was good at hearing things he wasn't meant to, and that people would pay for them. The trouble was that nobody at home had much worth paying for. So he left. There was no falling out and no great drama. He kissed his mother, shook his father's hand, and walked down out of the wind.

Dalaran. His first real job found him in the ruins of Dalaran, where the Kirin Tor were rebuilding behind their great violet dome. A man who never gave his name paid him to kill a magister named Corvel Ashgrave. Ashgrave had been one of the quiet ones in the years before the plague, a colleague of Kel'Thuzad who never quite stopped answering his old friend's letters. The Kirin Tor could not be seen to try one of their own, and the man with no name could not be seen to care. Anoures spent three weeks as a porter hauling stone for the masons, learning which stairways the wards didn't cover and when the magister took his tea. He did the job with a cup of that tea and was gone before it went cold. Only later did he learn that the man with no name had come from Ravenholdt Manor, and that the whole job had been a test. He had passed.

The trade. Since then he has worked for anyone with the coin: nobles and merchants, guild masters and generals, the shadowed halls of Ravenholdt and, more than once, Stormwind's SI:7. He is not loyal to any of them, and he has never pretended to be. That honesty is what makes him valuable. When Anoures takes a job, it gets done. His word is the one thing he has never sold, and everyone who has hired him knows it.

Not everyone has kept their own word in return. A baron in the Arathi Highlands tried to settle his bill with a crossbow bolt. A Westfall smuggler sold him to the Defias to clear a debt. A merchant prince in Booty Bay locked him in a hold and sailed. Each time, Anoures got out: over a roof, through a sewer grate, once by swimming two miles of shark water. Each time, he remembered. He doesn't take revenge, exactly. He simply makes sure that, someday, the right person learns the right thing about the people who crossed him.

The kites. Somewhere along the way Anoures stopped being a spy and became a spymaster. He keeps a web of informants across the Eastern Kingdoms that he calls his kites, after the paper toys children fly in the highland wind: they go up where he can't, and he holds the string. A Stormwind stable boy, a barmaid in Menethil, a gnome clerk who files the Ironforge customs ledgers, a Thelsamar fisherman with very good eyesight. None of them know each other. Most don't know who they really report to. Every scrap they send ends up in Anoures's head, cross-filed against everything else.

He loves the work. Not the coin, not the danger, the work itself: the patient joy of knowing something no one else knows, and of knowing where it fits. He wants to know everything about everyone. He is well on his way.

The money. His parents want for nothing now. He saw to that years ago, quietly, through a merchant they think is just very generous. The rest he hoards, and what he spends goes to the career: bribes, safe houses, new kites, the occasional favour bought from someone powerful and saved for later.

The six shadows

He jokes that a spy needs at least six shadows. These are the ones people have met:

  • Master Vicarious Thorne, a soft-spoken wine merchant from fallen Lordaeron, welcome at Stormwind noble houses (he never drinks his own stock).
  • Brother Sober, a wandering pilgrim of the Light who listens to confessions on the road.
  • Pell Lateral, a mapmaker and courier who is always "just passing through."
  • Jambi the Seer, a fortune teller in Darkshire whose predictions are uncannily accurate, because he already knows.
  • Fibon Ashe, a dry, forgettable accountant who audits other people's books.
  • Schism, a name whispered along the docks of Menethil and Booty Bay; no one agrees on what he looks like.

Some say Anoures Lachrymore is just the seventh. He has never said which one is real.

Personality

Anoures is charming, but quietly so. He isn't loud and he never tries to be the centre of the room. Left alone, he keeps to himself, watching, listening, nursing a cup of water and saying nothing. Speak to him, though, and he is warm, attentive and easy to talk to, with a way of making people feel they've been heard. They usually have, rather more thoroughly than they realise.

Underneath the charm he is as deadly as they come. He has no favourite weapon and no signature trick. Poison, blade, a forged letter, a word in the right ear: he uses whatever gets the job done, and he chooses the quietest tool that will do it.

He trusts no one. In his position he can't, and he doesn't pretend otherwise, not even to the kites who serve him. He fears nothing, or at least nobody has ever found the thing he fears, and plenty have looked.

He doesn't drink. A spy who gets drunk talks, and a spy who talks doesn't stay in work for long. He doesn't preach about it. He just orders water and smiles.

In character

  • He usually sits somewhere quiet with a clear view of the door and a glass of water. He'll politely decline any drink he's offered.
  • He rarely starts a conversation, but if you talk to him, he's friendly, curious and a very good listener.
  • He remembers small details about people (names, hometowns, who they were sitting with last week) and mentions them casually. It is meant to be noticed.
  • If he gives you his word, it is kept. If he won't give it, take note.
  • He might introduce himself by a different name than the last time you met. Don't take it personally.
  • He's heard a lot of names through his work, and he might know of you even if you've never met. Ironforge has an alchemist who keeps very odd hours, a Skyborne librarian who never stops lobbying, and a dwarf shaman with a bad knee who talks to the water. Stormwind has a priest who asks a lot of questions about the Defias, and a tailor up in the high apartments who keeps the lamps lit very late. The Arathi has a mercenary captain who never breaks a contract and, oddly enough, shares his surname. No relation, as far as either of them knows.
  • Ask him who he works for and he'll smile and say, "Whoever's paying." Ask him who he works for today, and he'll just smile.
