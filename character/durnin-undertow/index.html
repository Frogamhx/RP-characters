Appearance

Durnin is a broad, solid dwarf of about fifty, with greying hair and a thick grey-shot beard he keeps braided with small river stones and a few bands of tarnished copper. His hands are huge and calloused, but surprisingly careful. He dresses for the weather rather than for show: oiled leathers, a heavy blue-grey cloak, and boots that are almost always damp at the toes.

He walks with an easy, unhurried roll that hides a hitch in his left knee. On cold mornings, or before a storm, the limp shows more, and he'll stop on a bridge or a stair and lean on the rail as if he just wanted to admire the view. There's a faint smell of rain about him, even deep inside the mountain, and water near him tends to go very still.

Biography

For about twenty years, Durnin Undertow kept the Stonewrought Dam in Loch Modan. He wasn't a miner or a soldier. He was the dwarf who knew every sluice gate, pressure seam and hairline crack in that great wall, and he took quiet pride in the fact that the loch stayed exactly where dwarves had told it to stay.

Then the Cataclysm came.

He was down in the lower works when the mountain shook and the dam split open. Stone screamed, the lamps went out, and the whole loch came down on top of him. By every right, it should have killed him.

It didn't. The water went around him, then lifted him and carried him. While he was in it, he heard something. It wasn't words. It was closer to grief, the loch mourning as it poured out of the bed it had lain in for centuries. He woke on the mud flats miles downstream without a scratch on him, except for his left knee, which had been crushed against the stone on the way out. It has ached ever since.

He couldn't stop hearing the water after that. Rivers muttered, rain sighed, and the deep springs under Ironforge hummed in a voice only he seemed to notice. When the Earthen Ring came to Ironforge to teach the first dwarven shamans, Durnin was one of the first in line. He wasn't eager. He was desperate to understand what had spoken to him that night. He still carries some guilt about it: he spent twenty years holding that water back, and it saved him anyway.

Today he works for the Earthen Ring and for the city. He tends the springs, cisterns and cooling channels that run beneath Ironforge, and he travels often to Loch Modan to help coax the water back into its old home. The work keeps him away more than he'd like.

At home is Nicola, his wife. They never had children, and Durnin doesn't treat it as a sorrow so much as a fact: the two of them have always been enough for each other. He spends every hour he can with her, and when he's away, he writes her short, terrible letters about the weather that she keeps anyway. She is the one thing in his life he has never needed the elements to explain.

Personality

Durnin is gentle and laid-back, the sort of dwarf who lets an argument run its course and then quietly says the one thing that settles it. He's hard to rattle and slow to anger, and he has a habit of answering urgent questions with "Aye, well, water finds its way," which is either deep wisdom or a way to avoid the question.

The water taught him patience. It doesn't rush, but it never stops, and it wears down mountains in the end. He believes most problems work the same way. He has no use for shouting, and if someone wastes water or treats the elements carelessly, he'll correct them calmly and kindly, the first time.

Underneath the easy manner he is deeply loyal, especially to Nicola, and he never quite forgets that he owes his life to something he once spent decades trying to control.

In character

  • He's often found sitting by a fountain, channel or cistern in Ironforge with a whiskey sour, apparently doing nothing. He is listening.
  • He stretches his left knee before standing and grumbles that it's going to rain. He is usually right.
  • He knows Anoures Lumenor from around the city. They aren't friends, but they'd both say they know each other, and Durnin has been known to borrow a book on old waterways from the library.
  • He has seen Anoures Grudgebane in the Stonefire Tavern but has never spoken to him. He notices that the human always sits by the fire, and yet the ice in his whiskey never seems to melt. He hasn't decided what to make of it.
  • If you ask about the dam, he'll go quiet for a moment, then change the subject to Nicola.
