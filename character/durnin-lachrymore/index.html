Appearance

Durnin is a broad, solid dwarf of about fifty, with greying hair and a thick grey-shot beard he keeps braided with small river stones and a few bands of tarnished copper. His hands are huge and calloused, but surprisingly careful, and there's usually a half-whittled totem and a knife somewhere in his pockets. He dresses for the weather rather than for show: oiled leathers, a heavy blue-grey cloak, and boots that are almost always damp at the toes.

He moves with an easy, unhurried roll, and he's as fit and sturdy as any dwarf half his age. There's a faint smell of rain about him, even deep inside the mountain, and water near him tends to go very still. At his hip hangs whatever mace has served him best lately. He has no sentiment about weapons and swaps one out the moment he finds a better one.

Biography

For about twenty years, Durnin Lachrymore kept the Stonewrought Dam in Loch Modan. It wasn't a calling. He had never wanted to be anything in particular, and the job simply found him and suited him. He was the dwarf who knew every sluice gate, pressure seam and hairline crack in that great wall, and he took quiet pride in the fact that the loch stayed exactly where dwarves had told it to stay.

Then the Dark Irons came.

He was alone in the lower works one night when saboteurs blew a sluice gate. The dam held, but the tunnel he was standing in filled in a heartbeat. The lamps went out, stone groaned, and the loch came in on top of him. By every right, it should have drowned him.

It didn't. The water went around him, then lifted him and carried him out through the spillway as gently as a hand. While he was in it, he heard something. It wasn't words. It was closer to a voice, old and patient, and it seemed to know him after twenty years of keeping it. He woke on the bank below the dam without a scratch on him. The saboteurs were never caught, and Durnin has kept a quiet, unforgiving eye on the Dark Irons ever since.

He couldn't stop hearing the water after that. Rivers muttered, rain sighed, and the deep springs under Ironforge hummed in a voice only he seemed to notice. He didn't go looking for shamanism so much as drift into it, the way a stream finds its way downhill. He sought out the dwarven shamans, learned to carve his own totems, and found that every element answered him: earth, fire and air all work with him well enough. Water is simply the one that feels right in his hands.

Today he works for the city. Dwarves have come to accept shamans, and people ask him for help all the time: a spring gone sour, a flooded mine shaft, a well that won't fill, a farmer in Dun Morogh praying for rain. He tends the cisterns and cooling channels beneath Ironforge, and his work takes him on the road far more than he'd like.

It was on the road that he met Nicola. He stopped at her inn one wet night on his travels, stayed longer than he meant to, and kept finding reasons to come back. Now she keeps an inn in Ironforge, and the two of them have never had children. Durnin doesn't treat it as a sorrow so much as a fact: they have always been enough for each other. When he's away, he writes her short, terrible letters about the weather, and she keeps every one.

Personality

Durnin is gentle and laid-back, the sort of dwarf who lets an argument run its course and then quietly says the one thing that settles it. He's hard to rattle and slow to anger, and he answers urgent questions with "Aye, well, water finds its way," which is either deep wisdom or a way to avoid the question.

The water taught him patience. It doesn't rush, but it never stops, and it wears down mountains in the end. He believes most problems work the same way. He has no use for shouting, and if someone wastes water or treats the elements carelessly, he'll correct them calmly and kindly, the first time.

He isn't afraid of anything, and he'll tell you so without a hint of bragging. The only creatures that reliably get under his skin are gnomes. Pesky little buggers, always tinkering, always leaking oil into something, and forever asking to "just take a quick look" at his totems.

Underneath the easy manner he is deeply loyal, especially to Nicola, and he never quite forgets that he owes his life to something he once spent decades trying to control.

In character

  • Every morning starts with a shower. He rigged one himself, fed straight from a cold spring under the mountain. When he gets home at night, he kisses Nicola first, then showers again "to wash the day away."
  • A typical letter home: "Nicola. It's been raining the last three days here. It's glorious! Yours, D." She has a drawer full of them.
  • His favourite toast: "Deep wells and full cups." When someone is rushing him, he'll say, "Still water isn't sleeping, lad. It's thinking."
  • He's often found sitting by a fountain, channel or cistern in Ironforge with a whiskey sour, apparently doing nothing. He is listening.
  • He knows Lumenor Lachrymore from around the city. They aren't friends, but they'd both say they know each other, and Durnin has been known to borrow a book on old waterways from the library.
  • He has seen Grudas Lachrymore in the Stonefire Tavern but has never spoken to him. He notices that the human always sits by the fire, and yet the ice in his whiskey never seems to melt. He hasn't decided what to make of it.
  • If you ask about the dam, he'll go quiet for a moment, then ask whether you've heard any news of the Dark Irons.
