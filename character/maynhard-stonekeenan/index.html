Appearance

Maynhard is a broad, solid Skyborne in his early forties, with olive skin weathered by years of wind and road. His long hair is white with a cold blue tint, like frost on a highland morning, and his eyes carry the faint cyan glow of his people. His face is hard-set and rugged, and for a man who has fought most of his life he carries surprisingly few marks. There are no visible scars on him, which his brothers put down to luck and Maynhard puts down to a good shield. That shield is a heavy, spiked thing of the old style, and his sword is plain, well kept and clearly used. Neither has a name. He says names are for things you plan to lose.

Tucked somewhere on him, usually inside his gauntlet or knotted at his belt, is a small, faded cloth bearing the family crest: a gold leopard on a purple round field, two crossed swords behind it, ringed in gold trim.

Biography

The Stonekeenans have sold their swords for as long as anyone in the family can remember. Back in the high, windswept country of the Skyborne, the gold leopard meant a line that would not break: hire a Stonekeenan, and whatever was behind them stayed safe. The trade made the family wealthy, and the family was large. Maynhard grew up the middle of eight children, six brothers and two sisters, which meant there was always someone to fight and rarely a quiet moment to himself.

His father taught him the sword and shield in the yard behind the house. His mother taught him the rest. She never needed to work, but she spent her days giving it away anyway, tending the sick, feeding the poor and sitting with families who had lost someone to the kind of wars her husband was paid to fight. Maynhard learned from her that strength is something you owe to other people.

Of all his siblings, he is closest to his brother Danyel. The two were always paired off as boys, sparring partners, partners in trouble, and the only two in the house who could sit together without talking. Danyel is still the first person Maynhard writes to when something happens, good or bad.

At seventeen he took his first real contract, standing beside his father. The job was holding a mountain pass for a merchant house while raiders tried to force it. It was cold, long and frightening, and at the end of it his father put a hand on his shoulder and said only, "You held." Maynhard has been chasing that feeling ever since.

He could have stayed and fought alongside his family for the rest of his life. Instead he realised that he had spent his whole life defending other people's corners of the world without ever seeing any of it. So he left, not angrily, but with every one of his siblings seeing him off, Danyel walking with him the furthest.

Out in the world, he built a company of his own. The Stonekeenan way is strictly soldiering: he takes military work and nothing else. No thefts, no collecting debts and no knives in the dark. His clients are lords and wealthy houses with a problem that needs an armed answer, and Alliance commanders who need a hard, reliable unit for a particular kind of mission.

The job he still thinks about is Southshore. With Forsaken forces pressing out of Tarren Mill, his company was hired to hold the road into the town. They held it longer than anyone asked of them, until the order came to withdraw, and they withdrew. Southshore still stands, and by every measure the contract was honoured. Maynhard does not see it that way. He lost men on that road and gave it up, and he has never quite forgiven the retreat, even knowing it was the right call.

Keenan's Rise

He has settled, as much as a sellsword ever settles, in the Arathi Highlands. It is wide, windy, contested country, and it reminds him of home more than he will admit. His company holds a stretch of high ground there that the men call Keenan's Rise: a stockade, a training yard, a cluster of bunkhouses and, a little apart from the rest, Maynhard's own cabin.

He hasn't drifted from his family. He writes, he visits when he can, and he hears about every brother's wedding, every sister's quarrel and every niece's first sword. His parents are both still living, and his mother still sends letters asking whether he is eating properly.

He is. Away from the work, Maynhard fishes and cooks, often. On a quiet evening he is usually at the water with a line out, and his men have learned that a good catch means a good supper at the cabin. It is the one place he talks freely.

Personality

Maynhard is quiet. Years of growing up in a loud house left him comfortable in silence, and he tends to listen far more than he speaks. In a fight, and in most of life, he is focused and steady, the kind of man who notices the exit, the loose floorboard and the person in the corner who hasn't touched their drink. As far as anyone can tell, he fears nothing.

That doesn't mean he's grim. Get a few drinks into him, or put him at a table with people he trusts, and the middle child comes out: dry jokes, old family stories, and a laugh that surprises people who have only seen him working. He just doesn't waste it on strangers.

He is loyal to his family, to his employers for as long as the contract lasts, and to his word for longer than that. What he wants most is honor, earned the long way, one held line at a time. He thinks about the future more than he lets on, and the leopard on that faded cloth is a large part of why.

In character

  • He can usually be found with an ale or a mead in hand, whatever the house has. He isn't picky, only thirsty.
  • He sits with his back to the wall and his shield within reach, even when he's off duty.
  • Now and then he takes out the faded cloth and runs his thumb over the gold leopard before tucking it away again.
  • He mentions his brothers and sisters often and casually, but Danyel's name comes up more than the rest.
  • Offer him a fishing spot and a cooking fire and he'll happily lose an afternoon.
  • Mention Southshore and he goes quiet in a different way.
  • He doesn't say much, but when he does laugh, it's loud enough that people turn around.
