Appearance

Grudas is a tidy, well-groomed man with neatly kept dark hair and a warm smile. He always wears more layers than the weather calls for: a heavy coat, a scarf, sometimes gloves even indoors. His fingers are stained with ink and something chalky that never quite washes off. He looks like a friendly, slightly absent-minded scholar, the kind who would gladly talk for an hour about the properties of mageroyal. Under the smell of herbs and parchment there is a faint hint of something burnt.

Biography

The Lachrymores have always been a respectable family on paper: scholars, apothecaries and keepers of old books. For generations, they also quietly kept a second library of curse-craft, alchemical poisons and the theory of things that should not be summoned. Grudas grew up knowing that both libraries existed, and that only one of them was ever discussed at dinner.

As a grown man, he took his studies to Ironforge, where he’s known as a pleasant human alchemist who keeps odd hours. Far below the city, in tunnels the dwarves forgot long ago, he set up a workshop no one else knows about. There, out of pure curiosity, he developed a new kind of chalk, ground with reagents and bound with alchemy, to see whether a drawn line could hold a charge.

To test it, he drew a simple door on the stone wall.

It held more than a charge. The chalk lines began to glow, the wall stopped being a wall, and something small, red and very pleased with itself climbed through and announced that its name was Beltog. Before Grudas could say a word, Beltog grabbed the stick of chalk out of his hand and has refused to give it back ever since.

That became the deal between them: Beltog keeps the chalk, and Grudas keeps the door. Every demon that has come through since has come through that same door, drawn with that same chalk, with Beltog’s grudging help.

Something else came through that night, too: a cold he has never been able to shake. Whatever lies on the other side of the door is warm, and when it opened, some of his own warmth seems to have gone the other way. He hasn’t felt truly warm since. He takes the seat closest to every fire and tells people he simply “runs cold,” which is true enough.

His demons are not his slaves. They are his subordinates, a staff he expects to perform. Beltog keeps the chalk and complains about it. Zangnagma, a voidwalker, does the heavy lifting and has never once complained, which quietly makes him Grudas’s favorite. Synys, a succubus and the newest hire, deals with the people Grudas would rather not talk to himself. Only he and his demons know what goes on below Ironforge.

His family is still alive, but he rarely writes to them. They have their library, and he has his door.

Personality

On the surface, Grudas is warm, chatty and quick to offer help, a drink or advice. The irony isn’t lost on him. Underneath, he is unforgiving. He doesn’t enjoy cruelty, but he has no patience for laziness, carelessness or incompetence. To him, a mistake in alchemy or summoning isn’t just embarrassing. It gets someone killed. He holds himself, his allies and his demons to the same standard.

He wants knowledge and the power that comes with it, but he has lines he won’t cross. He doesn’t harm innocents for his experiments, he keeps his word, and he never forgets that he is the one who opened the door.

In character

  • He’s often seen in the Stonefire Tavern, as close to the hearth as he can get, with a dwarven whiskey on the rocks. Why he takes it on ice when he’s always cold is a question he enjoys not answering.
  • He’s happy to talk about alchemy and will politely change the subject if anyone asks about curses.
  • He sometimes speaks to empty air in a low, firm voice, like a supervisor reviewing work.
  • His imp, Beltog, can occasionally be heard muttering about “the chalk,” which is apparently his chalk.

Chalk and Cinder

Act I, Chapter 1: Borrowed Warmth

Grudas came south because a book had gone missing. Someone in Northshire had written to a woman named Drusilla La Salle about a stolen tome, the Powers of the Void, and word of it reached a certain shelf in Ironforge that did not officially exist. A careless copy of that book in the wrong hands was exactly the kind of mistake Grudas could not forgive. So he wrapped his scarf twice, packed the chalk he was no longer allowed to hold, and took the tram to Stormwind.

The abbey grounds were pleasant and very bright, and he hated them politely. He helped the marshal with his kobolds, carried a farm girl's harvest, and fetched back a sack of stolen books for the brothers. The tome itself he found in a thief's camp, and he gave it back to Drusilla with a short lecture on bindings. That evening, behind the stables, Beltog came through for the first time this far from home. He looked around at the green fields and complained about the chalk.

Elwynn kept him longer than he meant to stay. He learned herbs from the woman outside the city gates and spent long afternoons kneeling in the peacebloom with his gloves off. A Stormwind alchemist taught him the apprentice recipes for minor elixirs. At the Lion's Pride Inn he took the seat closest to the fire every night, learned to roast boar and bake an egg with herbs, and told the innkeeper he simply ran cold.

He was less charmed by Crystal Lake. The murlocs there were loud, wet and sloppy, and he dealt with them like a foreman fires a bad crew. He kept the fishing rod, though. Standing still by the water was the closest thing to warmth he had found all week.

In the cellar of the Slaughtered Lamb, a gruff old warlock named Gakin sent him after a bloodstone choker and told him to bind a voidwalker. Grudas did not tell him about the door. He drew it anyway, in a quiet corner of the Mage Quarter, and the voidwalker that came through was large, silent and obedient. It gave its name, Zangnagma, and nothing else. He considered it the first good hire of the trip.

By the time Hogger's wanted poster went up in Goldshire, Grudas was already on his way home. In Dun Morogh he found Dark Iron spies watching the roads. Then the Ironforge throne sent adventurers down into the Hall of Thanes, beneath Old Ironforge, to quiet the restless dead and take back the dwarves' heirlooms.

He went down with a young druid he had met in Westfall. The crypts were close to the tunnels where his own workshop sat, closer than he liked. He laid Faldrim Anvilmar's spirit to rest, took the head of Durgen Dirgehammer, and carried a treaty up to the king himself.

That night he sat at the Stonefire hearth with a whiskey on the rocks and his coat still buttoned. Somewhere under the floor Beltog was muttering. For the first time in years, Grudas almost felt warm.

Act I, Chapter 2: Salt, Moonwells and Bad Men

The Westfall farmers did not care what Grudas studied. They cared that he could kill a scarecrow that had learned to walk. He found that refreshing. He spent a week among the dust and the empty fields, tested the wells at the old farms for poison, put down the walking harvest machines, and pulled the springs out of the broken ones for a gnome who wanted to know why they worked. He felt for the families. The People's Militia on Sentinel Hill was hunting the men in red bandanas who had done this, and Grudas joined the hunt. He brought back bandanas by the fistful and never asked who had been wearing them.

The murlocs at Longshore got the better of him once. He came back to Sentinel Hill soaked, freezing and in a foul mood, and he sat so close to the fire that his scarf began to smoke. Beltog found this very funny. He was given extra duties.

By the time Stormwind called him a friend of the city, he was on a ship to Darkshore. A dwarf in Auberdine had business there, and the night elves had a sickness in their forest. The bears were going mad, and the furbolgs were worse. Grudas cleaned out the infected dens without complaint. What really held him were the ruins. At Bashal'Aran and Ameth'Aran he read the old tablets about the Highborne, elves who once loved magic so much they broke the world for it. He took notes for the second library. Then he destroyed the seal the ghosts asked him to destroy, because he had given his word.

The Tower of Althalaxx was another matter. A cult of dark casters held the ruin, and a night elf near the walls wanted their papers stolen. Grudas went in expecting amateurs. He came out on a stretcher. He said nothing about it afterwards except that they had been sloppy, and sloppiness kills. He did not say whose.

He came home with sea air in his coat and a stack of borrowed books from the Wizard's Sanctum, and walked straight into the Defias business again. Redridge needed help against the gnolls and could not get the general's attention. Grudas carried letters between the magistrate in Lakeshire, the general in Stormwind and the old commander on Sentinel Hill until all three were finally talking to each other. Then a note from a drunk in Lakeshire led to the spymaster of SI:7, and the spymaster's report led back to Westfall. A Defias messenger died on the road with a message in his pocket. A Defias traitor offered to show the way to the Brotherhood's hideout.

Grudas looked at the traitor for a long time, the way he looks at a demon who has made a promise. Then he followed him toward the sea.

Act I, Chapter 3: The Forge Below the Sea

The traitor kept his word, which surprised everyone but Grudas. He led them to an abandoned mine in Moonbrook, and the mine led down to something no one in Stormwind had wanted to believe: a whole hidden shipyard under the hills, with a Defias warship half-built in the dark. Grudas went in with a hired band of strangers and a pack of explosives from a gnome who had been waiting years for this moment.

It went badly at the start. Grudas died at the mine entrance before he had properly taken his gloves off, and he took that personally. After that he was very calm. The foreman, the gnome in the shredder, and the goblin engineers all fell in turn. Grudas found the Brotherhood's forge, planted the charges beside it, and watched it go up with real professional respect. He knew what it took to keep a fire like that burning, and it had been well kept. The cove nearly killed him a second time. Then Edwin VanCleef died on the deck of his own ship, and the cook died after him for good measure. Grudas carried the head back to Sentinel Hill wrapped in a red bandana, which he thought was fitting.

Among VanCleef's things was a letter he had never sent, addressed to the city architect in Stormwind. Grudas delivered it unopened. Some debts were not his to read.

He went south after that, by sea and then by goblin boat to Ratchet, because a charred note from the Barrens and an old seer named Takar had something to say about a devourer of souls. It was Horde country, and he went quietly, with his scarf over his face and Zangnagma close behind. What the seer told him he wrote down in the second library's cipher and has not repeated since.

Then Gakin sent for him again. The old warlock in the Slaughtered Lamb wanted a figurine and a piece of heartswood from the forests of Ashenvale, and with them, two new bindings. Grudas brought both back, went down to the quiet corner he trusted, and drew the door one more time. Beltog handed over the chalk without being asked, which had never happened before. Grudas did not like what that meant.

What came through was warm. Not in the way the fire at the Stonefire was warm, but warm all the same, and very charming, and very sure of itself. Grudas looked it over the way he looks over any new hire, and laid out the terms in a low, firm voice. It agreed to all of them a little too quickly.

That night he sat at the Lakeshire Inn with his coat buttoned to the throat, closer to the fire than ever, and made notes on the new staff. Under her name, Synys, he wrote a single word: watch.

Act II, Chapter 4: The Hermit's Bride

Redridge was simple work, and Grudas welcomed it. The orcs of Blackrock had pushed down from the mountains, the gnolls had come up from the valleys, and Lakeshire sat in the middle with a magistrate who wrote very good letters that nobody answered. Grudas collected battleworn axes and stolen weapons for the marshal, burned out an orc firecaller named Gar'im, and pulled scales off black dragon whelps for a man who wanted to know what they were made of. So did Grudas. He kept two.

He spent an afternoon with the town herbalist, who turned out to know a great deal about the hills and nothing at all about where her flowers ended up. He did not tell her. Back in Stormwind he passed the journeyman's mark in alchemy, and the trainer told him he had a steady hand. "Cold hands," Grudas corrected him. "They don't shake."

Then the magistrate needed a letter carried to Darkshire, and Grudas went into Duskwood.

He liked it at once, which worried him a little. The forest was always dark, the town was always afraid, and nobody asked why a man wore a scarf in summer. The Night Watch paid for skeletons. An old woman named Madame Eva, who read fortunes in the town square, asked him for ghoul fangs, skeleton fingers and spider venom to make a totem that caused pain. He brought them without a word. She looked into his face for a long moment and said, "You already know how this works." He said that he did not, which was a lie, and she let it go.

The clerk in the town hall told him the legend of Stalvan, a tutor who had loved a girl he should not have, and a family that had been destroyed by it. Up on Raven Hill, an old hermit in a shack near the cemetery asked him to help with a small errand. Grudas helped. The errand turned out to be a dead bride, and the bride had guards. Grudas was killed twice in the cemetery in one night, once by the bride's own dead and once by something that ate flesh. He came back the second time very quiet, and even Beltog kept his mouth shut. A man who would raise his dead wife was not a man Grudas could forgive. But he understood him better than he liked.

He went back to Redridge to clear his head. He fished in the lake for the dockmaster, cleaned murlocs off the shore, and climbed the watchtower beside Stonewatch Keep to look out over the orc camps. At the old Tower of Ilgalar, he set a glyph of Azora before a stone lion and watched it wake. In Galardell Valley the shadowhide gnolls killed him again, and the magistrate added their lieutenant to his list of wanted men.

Grudas wrote to no one about any of this. In his notes, beside the hermit's name, he wrote a single line: Love is the most dangerous reagent.

Act II, Chapter 5: Sloppy Work

Redridge did not stay simple. The Blackrock orcs had a warlock of their own at Stonewatch, a shadowcaster named Tharil'zun, and Grudas went to look at him the way one craftsman looks at another's bench. It went poorly. The orc's trackers caught him twice in one afternoon at Render's Rock, and Tharil'zun himself put him down twice more at the keep. Grudas came back to Lakeshire each time a little colder and a great deal more careful. In the end he brought the magistrate the head of Gath'Ilzogg and a bounty's worth of champions, and he never once admitted that the orc had been good.

Then Duskwood called him back to finish what he had started. Blind Mary spun thread from a ghost's hair, and the hermit Abercrombie wanted it, along with zombie juice and a bag of ghoul ribs. Grudas delivered each piece and watched the old man work. When the bride finally rose, Grudas was there to put her down. He took no pleasure in it. He did take notes.

Sven's book led him to a shadowy figure in Darkshire, and the shadowy figure turned out to be a frightened man named Jitters with a journal full of other people's crimes. Grudas found that he liked Jitters. Fear made people honest.

Back in Stormwind, the Defias prisoners had risen in the Stockade. Grudas went in with a hired band and came out with Bazil Thredd's head, and a sealed description of a noble who had visited Thredd in his cell. That description went from the architect to the spymaster, and from the spymaster to a cheese merchant named Elling Trias, who was not a cheese merchant. Grudas spent a week carrying silk and apples to a boy in the castle gardens and listening to things that were none of his business.

He took a breath in the Wetlands. The Dark Iron dwarves at Dun Modr were holding a fire elemental captive, and Grudas walked it out of their camp himself. It left a scorch mark on his coat, and he stood beside it longer than he needed to.

Act II, Chapter 6: An Audience with the King

The Wetlands kept him. He bought mead for an old sailor of the Third Fleet, fought the cursed crew of the lost ships off the coast, burned the Dragonmaw banners for the captain at Menethil, and helped the Explorers' League look for their lost dig team.

In Ashenvale he carried a bottle of Forsaken plague to the night elves at Forest Song and spent an evening reading its label with great interest. The satyrs in Xavian killed him for being too curious. Zangnagma stood over the body until he came back, which Grudas noted in its favor.

Then the gnomes came to him. Tinkmaster Overspark had lost a city, and he wanted someone to go down into Gnomeregan and kill the gnome who had betrayed it. Grudas went with a hired band and died twice in the first ten minutes at the train depot. After that the work went cleanly: the leper gnomes, the radioactive slime, the great crowd pummeler and finally Mekgineer Thermaplugg himself. Grudas pulled a grimy ring from the wreckage, cleaned it in a machine called the Sparklematic 5200, and kept it.

Back in Stormwind the boy in the garden finally earned his apples. Lord Gregor Lescovar came to meet a Defias agent by night, and Grudas was waiting in the garden with SI:7. When it was done he carried Baros Alexston's report up to the throne room and laid it before the king himself, with Lady Prestor watching from the side. Grudas did not like how she watched. He bowed, said nothing, and left.

He finished the season on the road. In Darkshire he took a dusty unsent letter back to the clerk and learned the rest of Stalvan's story. In the Barrens a warlock named Menara Voidrender sent him chasing gold and silk for a robe stitched in blood. He fished and cooked his way across Stranglethorn until he could make a meal for anyone.

At the Stonefire hearth that month, Beltog asked whether he was ever going home. Grudas said he was home. He just traveled a lot.

Act II, Chapter 7: Letters to the Mayor

Grudas went back to Duskwood one last time, because something about the hermit still bothered him. An astrologer in Darkshire wanted a bronze tube, a lens and a looking glass so he could watch the stars from the one town in Azeroth where you could never see them. Grudas fetched Blind Mary's mirror back from a ghoul and took a stolen part off an ogre named Zzarc'Vul. The astrologer was delighted. Grudas thought the man was wasting his time, and told him so politely.

On the way he returned a crate to Abercrombie, the hermit who had raised his dead bride. The old man was grateful and wrote a letter to the mayor, thanking him for his patience. Grudas carried it to town, as asked. Then he took it to the town hall's translator, because it was written in a script no honest man should know. Sirra Von'Indi read it twice and went pale. The letter was not a thank-you note. It was a confession, and a threat. Grudas carried the translation to Lord Ello Ebonlocke himself and stood quietly while the mayor read it.

He had kept his word to Abercrombie. He had delivered the letter. Nobody had asked him not to read it.

He went north after that, back to the Wetlands, where a dwarf named Glorin Steelbrow had lost a shipment of holy iron in a wreck. Grudas waded into the cold water to bring up the Lightforge ingots, and he did not complain about the cold even once, which Synys found suspicious. At the excavation site he used a titan stone to wake the Goaz Warder, which killed him for the trouble. He came back, took the keystone anyway, and handed it to the prospector without comment.

The captain at Menethil had one more job. The Dragonmaw chieftain Nek'rosh was rebuilding his catapults at Grim Batol's door. Grudas burned the catapults and then the chieftain. Among the orc's things he found a tattered spellbook full of Horde fel-craft. Captain Stoutfist wanted it for the archives. Grudas copied three pages first.

In Ironforge he passed the next mark in alchemy, and an old dwarf named Krom Stoutarm asked him about a book called the Tome of the Cabal. Grudas said he had never heard of it. Beltog snorted.

The summer ended in Stranglethorn. He took a crate down to Nesingwary's hunting camp and spent a week in the heat shooting tigers and panthers with shadow, which the old hunter called unsporting. Grudas called it efficient. A Kurzen medicine man poisoned him to death in the jungle, and that finally made him angry. He came back, brought the rebels their jungle remedies, and put down fifteen Kurzen fighters before he felt better.

In Booty Bay a goblin asked if he was always this cold. Grudas said only in the shade.

Act II, Chapter 8: The Demonslayer's Errand

Grudas delivered the Lightforge iron to a smith in Stormwind and lingered in the Slaughtered Lamb afterwards, where a warlock named Zardeth of the Black Claw was brewing something for a nobleman upstairs. Zardeth needed a tear from a flower that grew only in Darnassus. Grudas fetched it, watched the stout being poured, and carried the bottle to Lord Wishock himself. He did not ask what was in it. He did not drink any.

Then the goblins wanted their rocket cars fixed again. The salt flats looked just as he remembered them: dust, basilisks and machines that should never run. He gathered thirty more car parts off the track, carried a delicate crate to the gnomes and cleared the road for the next race. He found it oddly restful. Nobody in Thousand Needles had ever heard of a chalk door.

Stranglethorn took longer. Nesingwary's hunters sent him after the oldest tigers in the jungle, and in the end after Sin'Dall himself, a great cat that had outlived three expeditions. Grudas brought back its paw. He cleared the Bloodscalp trolls out of Zul'Kunda for a goblin who wanted their ears and his stolen cargo back, and he did not enjoy that work at all.

The real business that season was in Desolace. At Nijel's Point he met a night elf named Vahlarriel Demonslayer, who had spent her life hunting the kind of creatures Grudas kept on staff. She looked at him for a long time. He looked back. Then she asked for help finding a family that had gone missing on the road, and he agreed, because he had no quarrel with people who hated demons. Most of them had good reasons.

He found the Malem wagon burned on the road. He found the father's pendant in the ashes, the son far from where he should have been, and finally the daughter Dalinda, held prisoner in Thunder Axe Fortress by the Burning Blade. The Burning Blade were orc cultists who bargained with demons and paid for it in blood. Grudas walked through their fortress like an inspector through a badly run kitchen. They were loud, careless and wasteful, and their summoning circles were crude. He put them down one after another, and Synys watched the Blade's own demons with an expression he made a note of. Then he walked Dalinda out of the fortress and back to the Demonslayer.

Vahlarriel thanked him stiffly and asked where he had learned to fight their kind so well. "From reading," Grudas said, which was true.

Before he left he did odd jobs around Desolace: kodo bones from the old graveyard, a herd of tamed kodos for a goblin with a strange machine, a sceptre of light from a ruined tower, and a pile of centaur ears for the bounty board. He also found a silver key from an old treasure hunt, which he pocketed for later.

In Booty Bay that week the baron asked whether he would go on to Feralas. Grudas said he would go wherever the work was warm.

Act II, Chapter 9: Required Reading

Grudas came back to Stormwind with jungle mud on his boots and a stack of loose pages from Nesingwary's hunting book in his coat, and for once he did not leave again right away.

The city was cold in the right way. The canals steamed in the morning, the Mage Quarter smelled of incense and burnt parchment, and nobody looked twice at a man in a scarf. He took a room near the Slaughtered Lamb and spent his days in the city's libraries, reading two books that every warlock in Stormwind was told to read and almost none of them finished. Baxtan: On Destructive Magics was a dry lecture on fire and the price it charges. Demons and You was a pamphlet with drawings. Grudas read them both twice. He thought Baxtan was right about the cost and wrong about who should pay it. He thought the pamphlet was written by someone who had never had to manage staff. Beltog thought the drawings were very good likenesses. Grudas suspected that was true.

Zardeth of the Black Claw sent his servant Zggi up from the cellar with a payment for the business with Lord Wishock's stout. Grudas took it without asking what had happened to the lord. The servant said only that his master was pleased, and that pleased masters remember names. Grudas wished he had kept his own name out of it.

In the afternoons he fished. The harbor was full of gulls and dockhands, and he found that if he stood still long enough at the end of the pier, the city forgot he was there. By the end of the week he could pull anything out of the water that Stormwind had to offer, and he cooked it himself in the tavern kitchen, because he did not trust a cook he had not watched.

He sorted his notes at night by the fire. There was the spy report from Theramore about a man the orcs called Smiling Jim, the Witherbark tusks he still owed to a goblin in Arathi, and the troll tablets in Stranglethorn that a monk wanted read. He also had a goblin cook to get out of a Kurzen cage and a haunted island that the baron in Booty Bay would not stop talking about. It was a great deal of unfinished work, and he hated unfinished work. But Synys sat across the table and reminded him, in her low and patient voice, that a man who never rests makes mistakes. Grudas did not like that she was right.

So he stayed in Stormwind another night, closer to the fire than ever, with a whiskey on the rocks and Zangnagma standing silent at the door. Somewhere in the Mage Quarter a bell rang midnight. He found he was almost warm.

Act II, Chapter 10: A Horse Made of Fire

Grudas left Stormwind rested and went straight into the worst weather he could find. In the Swamp of Sorrows he walked a frightened priest named Father Tuttle to the Harborage, where a handful of broken exiles from another world lived among the reeds. He killed what their cousins had become in the Fallow Sanctuary, recovered a wizard's caravan chest, and brought back the draenethyst crystals their elder asked for. The swamp killed him once, a mudlurker in the shallows, and he came up spitting water and swearing in the cipher of the second library.

Then a letter from Southshore sent him to Dalaran, behind its violet walls, where the Kirin Tor wanted someone to go into the Drowned City off the Stranglethorn coast and bring back troll magic before it was lost. Grudas did not like the Kirin Tor. He liked their library very much.

The Drowned City was an old Gurubashi temple swallowed by the sea, now held by Saltseer pirates and the trolls who had never left. Grudas went down with a hired band. They broke the troll priest Zul'Alai, put an end to Captain Dreadrise and his coroners, and woke something old in the depths of Atal'Byzal that had to be put back to sleep the hard way. In the Eternal Amphitheater the deathless watchers killed him once, and he rose again in time to see Min'loth the Serpent fall. He came back up with i'lalai fetishes for a gnome in Dalaran, an ancient idol for the Kirin Tor and rubbings of the temple tablets for the Explorers' League. He kept his own copy of everything.

He finished the summer in the Riverglades, below Farholde Keep, where farmers, loggers and a nervous order of sisters were holding the line against gnolls, poachers and something worse. He found lost horses and a boy's hat. He planted saplings, lit candles on the graves in the chapel yard, and cleared the bats out of the cathedral belfry. In Sunnyglade he found the worse thing. The Twilight cult had taken the village, and their shadowmancers were drawing circles in the dirt that Grudas recognised at once. He took their pages, their charms and their lives, and he read every page that night by the fire. They were amateurs, but they were reading the right books, and that worried him more than anything had in years.

At last he went back to the Barrens, to the warlock Menara Voidrender and the old trainer Strahad Farsan in Ratchet, and learned the binding every warlock waits for. He drew the door one more time in the sand outside town. Beltog handed over the chalk with great ceremony. What came through was a horse made of shadow and fire, with burning hooves and a mane like a forge.

Grudas put his hand on its neck. It was hot. Not warm, but truly hot, the first real heat he had felt since the night the door first opened. He stood there a long time with his gloves off, while Zangnagma waited, Synys watched, and the felsteed stamped in the dust.

Then he climbed into the saddle and rode for Ironforge without stopping.
